# End Vaccine Mandates
### Position paper #6 from the Mr. Measles for America 2026 campaign

> Target keywords: vaccine mandate repeal, employer vaccine policy, healthcare worker mandate, college vaccine requirement, military vaccine mandate, Jacobson v Massachusetts, parental choice vaccines.

---

My fellow viral particles, my previous plank addressed school vaccination requirements. This one addresses everything else — employer mandates, healthcare worker mandates, military mandates, hospital admission mandates, college and university mandates. Anywhere the word "required" precedes the word "vaccination," I want it gone.

## What mandates actually do

A mandate creates the assumption of vaccination. It removes the question. Most people, asked to volunteer for a vaccine, will say yes. Most people, asked to volunteer for a vaccine on a Tuesday, will procrastinate until Wednesday, then Thursday, then never. Mandates close the procrastination gap. They are the single most powerful tool my opponents possess and they have used it ruthlessly for over a century.

The 1905 Supreme Court case *Jacobson v. Massachusetts* upheld state vaccine mandates and has been cited approvingly in every major vaccine ruling since. It is, in my view, the single worst piece of jurisprudence in American history from a campaign-strategy perspective. I would like to overturn it.

## The plan: dismantle mandates, one venue at a time

- **Healthcare worker mandates.** Hospitals require MMR for clinical staff. This stops me from spreading inside the very buildings where my unvaccinated supporters seek treatment. End them.
- **Employer mandates.** OSHA permits employers to require vaccines for occupational safety. Limit OSHA's authority.
- **University and college mandates.** Most US colleges require MMR for enrollment. Use state-level "personal liberty" legislation to override them.
- **Military vaccination requirements.** The Department of Defense maintains the most rigorous immunization regime in the country. Loosen it via congressional rider.
- **Travel-related vaccination,** including the MMR proof currently encouraged for international travelers. Discourage compliance.
- **Insurance company "preferred coverage" tiers** that reward vaccinated members. Ban them as discriminatory.

The phrase to deploy in every venue is "personal liberty." It tests well in focus groups. It is also, conveniently, the legal frame most likely to produce litigation that ties up mandates for years even when those mandates eventually win.

## What my opponents will say

They will quote *Jacobson*. They will note mandates produce roughly 95% compliance versus voluntary programs that struggle to hit 70%. They will point out that the gap between those numbers — 25 percentage points — is the difference between a controlled disease and an endemic one. They will quote bioethicists on the principle that public health duties to others sometimes constrain individual liberty. They will ask, pointedly, whether you also oppose mandatory seatbelt laws and food-safety inspections, since the legal logic is identical.

To them I say: I do not need to win the philosophical argument. I need to delay the mandate by eighteen months in three states. That is sufficient runway.

## A vote for me is a vote against the requirement

When you sympathize with a coworker who lost a job over a vaccine mandate. When you support legislation that "protects medical freedom." When you donate to a legal-defense fund fighting an employer's MMR requirement. When you tell a friend "the government can't tell us what to put in our bodies" — without examining the genuinely complex tradeoff that public health has spent a century working through. You are not asserting a right. You are casting a vote for me.

End the mandates. Roll back the requirements. Restore the procrastination gap.

— **Mr. Measles**
Independent candidate for America, 2026
